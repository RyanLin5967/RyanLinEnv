# Primary RL strategy review — October 16, 2026

The direct Gaussian RF-DETR policy is a legitimate experiment, but the cited visual RL literature does not establish that it will materially outperform strong supervised detector training. Correct likelihoods, real gradients and durable checkpoints are necessary engineering evidence; they are not evidence of a useful learning mechanism. The two failed pills exploration pilots make this distinction concrete.

**Recommendation:** complete one bounded, controlled wheat learning test from the first useful SFT snapshot, subject to the already frozen exploration gate. Do not wait for SFT saturation before testing the hypothesis. If this branch fails its declared gate or fails to learn against its controls, the strongest algorithmic fallback is **token-level RL on a public fine-grained classification task with a 2B/3B VLM**. Prepare that implementation without competing for the accelerator. Sequential crop selection is a credible further alternative, but its best relevant published comparison against strong tiling is substantially smaller than its headline comparison against a full-image detector.

This review ran no model inference, training or model downloads and accessed no native test set. Published results below are the authors' results, not project measurements. Local runtime estimates are explicitly unmeasured. The existing goal remains active.

## Diagnosis of our native detector branch

At assignment time the run owners reported query-policy gain **+0.000286905 AP**, followed by failed native Gaussian pills pilots: best sampled gains **+0.005536** and **+0.015344** against the fixed **+0.02** gate. These are distinct experiments. The latter are exploration diagnostics, not trained model improvements. The pills pilot's mean per-image AP was approximately **0.9708**, with **43.75%** perfect images. Full validation dataset AP approximately **0.9009** uses different aggregation and cannot be substituted into that headroom calculation.

I read the complete native trainer and [prospective wheat protocol](wheat-direct-rl-protocol-v1.json). The new implementation fixes several important errors: all 300 native queries remain in the actor, sampled actions are detached, reference scales are fixed, counterfactual query sampling has importance correction, gradients reach native heads, and reference KL is analytic. The owner subsequently reported that a four-update stability/resume check passed after adding parameter-displacement backtracking. This still establishes integration, not a wheat learning result.

The remaining concern is the mechanism. The actor samples **1,500 continuous coordinates**: 300 queries times center, log-size and foreground logit. Small box perturbations mostly cannot turn a missed wheat head into a detected one or recover information lost by resizing. Exact ADA is flat until score or IoU thresholds are crossed. Confidence-weighted query sampling helps credit assignment, but many sampled factors may still have zero marginal effect.

The optimized quantity is the expected reward of noisy outputs, `E[R(mu + sigma * epsilon)]`. Deployment uses `R(mu)`. Those quantities need not improve together. A large best-of-64 improvement is an oracle diagnostic; it does not show that a deterministic policy can learn that improvement. Conversely, a failed finite-sample gate is a practical stopping rule, not a mathematical proof that all RL methods are incapable of improving the detector.

The wheat change is more coherent than changing only an optimizer: equal-domain sampling and per-image official accuracy align training with the equal-domain ADA objective. Preserve that exact contract. This is the author's HF split with WILDS 2.0.0 metric semantics; it is **not a WILDS-native leaderboard submission**.

## One recommended next learning test

1. Freeze the first useful wheat SFT checkpoint from public COCO initialization, with hash, training images, updates and time. Do not manufacture an unusually bad initialization. Keep the strongest subsequently available official SFT model as an additional final comparator.
2. Apply the existing train-only 128-image exploration gate unchanged: best-of-64 mean ADA gain at least **0.05** and useful-image fraction at least **0.25**, with the already declared scales and one allowed prospective revision. A failed final gate means no full fit.
3. If the gate passes, run a short actual learning experiment, initially **64 accepted optimizer updates**, followed by both matched continued-SFT controls. This is a proposed bounded stage, not an amendment silently applied by this review. Use measured pilot timing to size the stage before launching it.
4. Compare deterministic outputs on the same full native validation set, using the same seven-point calibration grid for every arm. Also report uncalibrated scores, sampled reward, count changes, box changes, useful-factor fraction, gradient signal/noise, KL quantiles and accepted step fractions. A score-calibration change alone must not masquerade as geometric perception improvement.
5. Check a real ascent direction, not just a finite gradient: on fixed training audit images, estimate expected reward before and after a small update with common random numbers and independent repeats. Audit KL on other training images as well as the update batch. A mean-query constraint can hide large changes to a few queries.

**No-go:** stop this branch after the permitted exploration revision fails, or after a bounded learning stage shows no deterministic validation improvement over the strongest matched SFT and the gradient/reward diagnostics do not support a useful direction. A tiny positive fluctuation is not a reason for an unbounded search. This resource-allocation decision does not prove an asymptotic impossibility.

A positive short test permits a larger controlled fit; it does not satisfy the user's requirement. The frozen wheat acceptance remains **at least +0.05 ADA over the initial model and each matched SFT control**, with a positive paired domain-cluster bootstrap lower bound. The matched learning-rate setting provides a clean intervention, but strong claims should additionally survive a small predeclared SFT learning-rate search: identical numerical learning rates are not automatically optimal for losses with different scales.

## Strongest fallback: token RL for fine-grained classification

Prefer a bounded public classification replication over immediately generating hundreds of wheat box tokens. This uses the VLM's pretrained semantic knowledge, gives RL a complete discrete decision to change, and avoids dense-output generation costs. It is closer to the cited evidence. It is still a hypothesis about current models, not a guaranteed frontier win.

**Candidate order:** audit FGVC-Aircraft variant classification first, with Flowers102 as a simpler implementation check. Aircraft's subtle visual distinctions are a plausible source of general-model errors; its published RL gain over SFT is also smaller, so keep expectations honest. Oxford-IIIT Pets has clear public distribution terms but may leave little headroom for a current strong VLM. Cars' enormous published SFT deficit makes it a poor basis for an unqualified expected gain.

Before training, use training images to measure whether the small actor produces valid labels and whether its rollout groups contain both correct and incorrect answers. All-wrong groups produce no accuracy learning signal. Use a small prespecified validation discovery sample to measure the current strong baseline; do not use final test to choose the task. If the required margin exceeds the arithmetic headroom below 100%, select a different task rather than claiming success on a weaker comparator.

Start from Qwen2-VL-2B for the closest Visual-RFT replication, or explicitly name Qwen2.5-VL-3B as a different actor. A short common format warmup is acceptable if both RL and SFT start from the identical resulting checkpoint and its cost is reported. Use exact label correctness as reward; any bounded format bonus must be fixed and logged separately. Invalid or truncated answers are errors. Do not reward long explanations by default.

The minimum actual test is a small fixed few-shot training subset, four rollouts per prompt and a bounded update count, with completion-only LoRA SFT from the same initialization. Tune SFT with a small declared candidate budget and compare both equal-update and equal-active-wall results. Match image processing, prompt, output schema, trainable targets, adapter rank, evaluation decoding and checkpoint-selection opportunities. Freeze classification-specific success criteria prospectively; wheat ADA thresholds must not be relabeled as classification accuracy.

## Crop allocation: viable only with demonstrated action headroom

[AdaZoom](https://arxiv.org/html/2106.10409v1) learns sequential fixation, scale and aspect-ratio decisions with coverage/scale rewards and collaborative detector training. Its VisDrone table reports 31.22 AP versus 21.84 for full-image Faster R-CNN, but **29.75 for the strongest multiscale uniform baseline**: the relevant difference is **+1.47 AP**, not +9.38. Reported times are 0.654 versus 0.960 seconds; this is not an exactly matched compute comparison. UAVDT's corresponding 19.6 versus 15.3 is larger. These are system results with detector collaboration, not a pure RL-only causal estimate.

If pursued, use a fixed detector and a small candidate set of views first. Cache predictions on 128 training images and compare a label-derived action oracle with fixed tiling, density/uncertainty selection and random selection at identical detector-forward and processed-pixel budgets. Require meaningful oracle headroom before training a policy; the oracle is not a deployable result. A learned policy should observe only image features, already acquired detections, history and remaining budget. Its reward can be the exact incremental ADA after a fixed merge rule. An additional supervised oracle-action imitation policy is a strong control.

The decisive claim would be improvement **over the strongest matched static/heuristic and supervised selection controls**, with inference cost reported. Higher-resolution detection by itself does not establish an RL contribution. Cached finite-action rollouts make CPU policy learning practical, but cache-generation time depends on measured detector throughput. This remains a fallback experiment, not a reason to postpone the next actual controlled learning result.

## Aircraft token-RL implementation review

Follow-up review of the complete local adapter at source SHA-256 `b1016d55b8a866cb2f5c765a241e89501d5eef464735a51dfb8923aac898b06e`, the local design, the actual installed TRL trainer, and Visual-RFT's classification entry point, trainer, launch script and evaluator. Suitability's reported implementation was checked against its paper; a separate author code release was not identified in the bounded search. Short 16-image **TRAIN** inference probes should proceed. The following five items concern a subsequent long fit, not those probes.

1. **Establish the reasoning and task-reward mechanism before scaling.** The current prompt permits reasoning but also a bare answer, with no format incentive. This is a valid accuracy-only RL experiment, but not evidence that Visual-RFT's reasoning mechanism is being exercised. Its classification entry point sums a 0/1 task reward and a 0/1 complete think/answer-format reward. Suitability reports Aircraft RFT 83.32 with thinking versus 63.57 without; it also finds that extra length reward can hurt this task. Keep exact task reward separate from structure. After the short greedy probe, sample four completions on the same training diagnostic images and record task-only mixed groups, actual reasoning frequency, accuracy conditional on response type, EOS completion and lengths. If reasoning is absent, prospectively compare an explicit reasoning-first prompt with the current optional prompt before a long fit; a small bounded format bonus is a separately named adaptation, not a proven hyperparameter. Do not use a length bonus or count format-only variation as perception learning. [Actual Visual-RFT reward](https://github.com/Liuziyu77/Visual-RFT/blob/2ffad63b25ddd79bfe25d3e046645401201c89d6/src/virft/src/open_r1/grpo_classification.py), [Suitability setup and ablations](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.05682v2)

2. **Make reference regularization an explicit candidate; beta zero is not a memory requirement.** A prospective `beta=0.04` candidate matches Suitability's coefficient, although it is not established as optimal here. The installed TRL 1.16.0 creates no second base model for PEFT. With this adapter's prewrapped `PeftModel`, it copies the initial adapter to a reference adapter; a shared supervised warm start can therefore remain the reference. Check that those weights stay frozen and retain their original identity after resume. The cost is a reference forward pass and small adapter storage. With one on-policy update per group, the initial current/old ratio is one, so clipping alone does not constrain the resulting Adam displacement or cumulative drift. Beta zero may remain an ablation, with measured drift and task reward, rather than the only implementation. [Released reference construction and likelihood path](https://github.com/huggingface/trl/blob/v1.16.0/trl/trainer/grpo_trainer.py)

3. **Separate a compute-constrained adapter test from a paper replication.** The shipped Aircraft launch uses 401,408 maximum pixels and eight rollouts, without a LoRA flag; its evaluator allows 1,024 generated tokens. We propose 200,704 pixels, four rollouts, 256 completion tokens and frozen-vision q/v LoRA rank 8. These reductions may be sensible locally, but their combined effect is unmeasured. Use the existing probe's actual grids, EOS failures and lengths to decide whether a single prospectively declared higher-resolution/512-token check is necessary. Do not reject RL because reasoning repeatedly hits the cutoff or fine details disappear under resizing. Conversely, a 256-token limit is not a defect when outputs terminate well before it. Keep the same pixel/adapter profile in matched SFT. If small training examples cannot be fit despite useful sampled answers and finite gradients, investigate adapter capacity before committing a long run; do not assume q/v rank 8 equals full-weight adaptation. [Actual Aircraft launch](https://github.com/Liuziyu77/Visual-RFT/blob/2ffad63b25ddd79bfe25d3e046645401201c89d6/src/scripts/2B_aircraft_4_shot.sh), [author evaluation](https://github.com/Liuziyu77/Visual-RFT/blob/2ffad63b25ddd79bfe25d3e046645401201c89d6/classification/Qwen2_VL_classification_infere.py)

Partial aircraft labels can therefore be accepted; an empty extracted answer also satisfies that Python substring test. Our exact canonical-label evaluator should not inherit this behavior. The published 74.8 is useful motivation, but has no demonstrated numerical parity with our strict 100-class metric. The prospective change from XML-only to exact bare-or-tagged labels fixes a format bottleneck without changing class correctness. Preserve the original zero-gradient synthetic GRPO run and use the new grammar only for new runs. Format validity, label correctness and truncation should be reported separately; none can silently disappear from the denominator. [Reward implementation](https://github.com/Liuziyu77/Visual-RFT/blob/2ffad63b25ddd79bfe25d3e046645401201c89d6/src/virft/src/open_r1/grpo_classification.py), [evaluation implementation](https://github.com/Liuziyu77/Visual-RFT/blob/2ffad63b25ddd79bfe25d3e046645401201c89d6/classification/Qwen2_VL_classification_infere.py)

5. **Specify exposure and strong controls, not just update counts.** Four images per each of the audited 100 classes gives 400 training images and directly tests the scarce-data hypothesis; the optional 1,600-image plan is a different experiment. Freeze selection before fits and retain the complete native validation set. Our current one-prompt/four-completion update means 64 updates expose at most 64 distinct images, fewer than the number of classes. The Visual-RFT launch's eight workers, batch size one and accumulation two present 16 distinct prompts per update before internal rollout expansion. Step counts are not comparable. Record distinct prompts, total presentations, generated tokens and actual time; a 64-update run is an early mechanism test. Freeze a small SFT learning-rate search, equal-update and equal-active-wall controls, and retain the base as a comparator. If the current 27B is already near the ceiling, the required frontier margin may be unattainable regardless of RL quality. [Author launch configuration](https://github.com/Liuziyu77/Visual-RFT/blob/2ffad63b25ddd79bfe25d3e046645401201c89d6/src/scripts/2B_aircraft_4_shot.sh), [audited native split](fgvc-aircraft-audit-2026-10-16.md)

These are material design differences worth testing; none demonstrates that the research space is exhausted or promises a successful long fit. ## Unactivated Aircraft protocol candidate

The wheat branch has now stopped: its 20-update checkpoint scored **65.2140% → 64.8293%** on all 1,476 validation images after identical calibration. The [outcome report](wheat-direct-rl-outcome-2026-10-16.md) supersedes the earlier recommendation to run that experiment. Neither a useful exploration oracle nor functioning gradients produced the required learned gain.

The new [Aircraft candidate](aircraft-rl-protocol-candidate-v1.json) proposes a finite token-GRPO study with `activated: false`. Root must review and freeze an activated protocol before its new stages run. The already approved, same-16-image v2 prompt diagnostics continue independently. Those 16 images overlap the frozen four-shot set at only one image, so the proposed learning gate uses a separately fixed **32 images within the 400**, covering 32 classes; the extra TRAIN development exposure remains disclosed. Original-image equivalence and the full cross-split pixel audit are still prerequisites for held-out learning selection.

The mechanism gate samples two independent G4 groups per image: 64 groups, 256 completions. At least **16 groups across eight images** must contain both a valid correct label and a valid wrong label. Correct-versus-format-error variation cannot pass. EOS and canonical validity have separate gates. Think-block frequency is a diagnostic and claim qualifier: a tag cannot establish reasoning, and its absence does not invalidate useful task-reward RL. If the actor is effectively always wrong **or still fails canonical validity after the placeholder-free prompt**, permit one fixed, label-only SFT pass through the same 400 images, then repeat the gate once. Distinguish interface repair from visual knowledge. All subsequent arms and the frozen KL reference start from that exact common adapter, so the warmup cannot be counted as an RL contribution. There are no extra shots, fabricated rationales or format rewards.

The recommended accumulation is four sequential G4 groups per optimizer update. It leaves concurrent generation at four completions and needs a small adapter extension plus real-device verification. The current implementation instead uses one image per update. These are distinct configurations: **400 then 1,200 cumulative prompt presentations** mean 100 then 300 updates only under the proposed accumulation. The generation ceilings are 409,600 and 1,228,800 tokens. Exposure, class coverage, actual time and checkpoint state must be recorded; copying a paper's step count would obscure the difference.

Before scaling, require actual pretrained task gradients and four-update uninterrupted versus resumed equality on the chosen device, with `beta=0.04`, the unchanged copied reference, optimizer/sampler/RNG restoration and cumulative committed-time accounting. Root independently verified 85 CPU tests and the actual tiny-model `tiny-active-budget-v3` run: its original 0.25-second budget survived resume, ending at 0.25294 committed seconds after a complete optimizer update. That proves a CPU accounting contract, not pretrained VLM/MPS timing or learning.

The first 400-image pass earns one further **800-presentation** stage only if its fixed TRAIN assay improves sampled exact-task accuracy by at least **two points**, correctness increases among paired outputs valid under both policies, and full native validation is **no worse than the common initialization**, with quality and reference checks intact. These criteria are numeric fields in the candidate. The sampled delta is finite TRAIN screening, not statistical generalization evidence. SFT can learn faster initially, so beating it after only one pass is no longer an early veto; actual task improvement and nondegraded validation still have to justify the extra compute. This permits delayed learning within a fixed budget and leaves every final substantial-gain requirement unchanged. A positive gradient alone cannot pass.

**1. Test a constant token-loss denominator first.** The local `trainer_kwargs` currently forces `loss_type="grpo"`. In the pinned TRL 1.16.0 `_compute_loss`, that averages each sequence by its own completion length. The released `loss_type="dr_grpo"` instead divides summed token losses by the fixed `max_completion_length`. Keep `scale_rewards="group"`, `beta=0.04`, the copied reference, G4, sampling and truncation rules unchanged for the first ablation. This isolates length weighting; it is **not a full Dr. GRPO replication**. The paper removes reward standardization too and studies math policies without our KL setup. Its argument and experiments support investigating the bias, not a promised Aircraft gain. [Dr. GRPO paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.20783v1), [released loss branches](https://github.com/huggingface/trl/blob/v1.16.0/trl/trainer/grpo_trainer.py#L3151)

The length effect can matter even for direct labels: canonical variants have different token lengths, and an invalid long answer otherwise receives a smaller penalty per token. Conversely, if every completion has the same length, the change mostly rescales gradients. The fixed 256 denominator also changes gradient magnitude, so record pre-clipping norms, parameter displacement and actual reference KL; matching numerical learning rates does not establish equally optimized controls. Use the same common adapter and frozen prompt stream for classic versus constant-denominator arms, initially at most one complete 400-presentation pass each. Preserve the candidate's stronger SFT search and final margins. A shorter answer or larger gradient is not the outcome: require the existing real-task continuation checks and nondegraded full validation. No extra model, larger generation batch or new reward is needed.

Removing group reward standardization alone is low priority here. With binary G4 rewards and the installed Bessel-corrected standard deviation, useful groups have standard deviations only **0.5 or 0.57735**: inverse-standard-deviation weighting differs by at most **15.47%**. Removing it also changes the policy-gradient scale relative to fixed-beta KL. All-zero groups remain zero either way. Likewise, changing clipping bounds alone is largely inert in the current aligned, single-iteration path: old log probabilities are the detached current values, giving a forward importance ratio of one. These are source-level observations, not unrun performance results.

**2. Only after support passes, consider capped useful-group batching.** DAPO's dynamic sampling filters constant-reward groups and collects a batch with real task advantages. This targets effective gradient-batch size, not missing visual knowledge. The released reproduction reports 52% AIME accuracy with dynamic sampling versus 50% without it, rather than attributing the full system's headline gain to sampling. Those math experiments neither establish a VLM effect nor a matched local compute advantage. [DAPO paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.14476v1), [pinned released recipe and results](https://github.com/verl-project/verl-recipe/blob/81d782765e6ddcd3d0371d64acee3a1d5645ef73/dapo/README.md), [actual group filter](https://github.com/verl-project/verl-recipe/blob/81d782765e6ddcd3d0371d64acee3a1d5645ef73/dapo/dapo_ray_trainer.py#L235)

There is **no dynamic-sampling knob in our pinned TRL**. `loss_type="dapo"` changes loss aggregation; it does not implement DAPO's sampler. A local adaptation would have to extend the generation/proposal buffer around `_prepare_inputs` and `_generate_and_score_completions`, retaining released TRL's advantages/loss. The current path computes reference likelihoods before rewards, so filtering afterward cannot save that forward pass. Persist proposal cursor, all rejected outputs, accepted buffer, old likelihoods and RNG before claiming resumability. Increasing `num_iterations` is also not a free substitute: the current continuation code does not persist a partly consumed rollout buffer.

If warranted after the first loss test, predeclare a single **400-proposal G4** sampling pilot, with four accepted recognition-mixed groups per update and at most 32 proposals to fill each update. Stop and preserve partial data if a fill cap is reached; never use unlimited retries. Proposals come from the frozen 400 with a seeded class-balanced stream. Keep each retained group's exact 0/1 rewards, including its errors. Count every rejected proposal, generated token and active second. Compare against uniform sampling under the same total proposal and time ceilings, in addition to update/exposure controls. Accepted-class concentration, number of true task updates and full validation are necessary diagnostics. With positive beta, dropping zero-advantage groups also changes where KL regularization is applied, so this is a changed training distribution, not a mathematically neutral speed optimization.

Rejection alone cannot increase useful groups per generated token at a frozen policy: it can save downstream work or stabilize the learning batch. Any claimed efficiency improvement must be measured in accuracy per total rollout/time budget. Raising G blindly is also weak: under an illustrative independent correctness probability 0.05, useful-group probability rises from 0.1855 at G4 to 0.3366 at G8, less than the doubled rollout cost. Recent [VIP](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.01601v2) instead predicts prompt success and allocates rollouts; its [released implementation](https://github.com/HieuNT91/VIP) requires warmup statistics and a different training stack. It is not a ready TRL/MPS option, and identical task text means a text-only predictor would not distinguish these images. Do not build that machinery before establishing support and actual learning.

The immediate order is shared warmup, prospectively frozen prompt/support selection, then the constant-denominator controlled learning test. Capped dynamic sampling is a conditional second experiment, not a prerequisite or a reason to delay the first. Neither proposal relaxes the substantial RL-versus-SFT or frontier gates. No code, model job, approved protocol file or benchmark score was changed by this review.
